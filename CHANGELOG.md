# Changelog

Each release's section here becomes its "What's new" on GitHub. Add the next one as
`## <version>` before tagging (see [Releasing](README.md#releasing)).

## Unreleased

- **Choose who never gets an away reply**: `away.skip` in `config.json` lists chats (nickname, @username or id) that never get an away reply, such as a manager, whatever their other rules. Sending, watching and auto-reply there are unchanged. On the tray's Away tab, **Who gets away replies** lists every saved person with a switch. Renaming a contact keeps them on the list, and a name only `away.skip` mentions can't be given to another chat, the same guard the rule lists have. The macOS menu bar's Away page doesn't show the list yet but keeps it when it saves.

## 1.2.0

- **Heads-up: a fresh `away on` now replies around the clock**: on a new install, or a `config.json` with no `away`, switching away on turns on both **Daytime** (08:00–21:00) and **Night** (21:00–08:00), so someone who writes gets a reply at any hour. Under `defaultPolicy: "approve"` (the default) that includes strangers, chats on none of your lists, as away replies already did at night. Switch off the schedule you don't want on the tray's Away tab, or set `defaultPolicy` to `"block"` so only chats on your lists get away replies. An older `config.json` with its own away hours keeps them.
- **Away schedules, with a wait that lets you answer first**: the away auto-reply now runs on schedules, each with its own hours, days of the week, optional start and end dates, wait (0 to 600 seconds), repeat and message, up to 12. Out of the box: **Daytime** 08:00–21:00 waits 30 seconds and then thanks them for getting in touch and says you'll reply when you're back at your seat (again after an hour if they write again), and **Night** 21:00–08:00 says at once that you're done for the day and will get back to them tomorrow. If you read a chat or send a message yourself on any device during the wait, the reply is dropped. When schedules overlap, the more specific one replies (dated first, then the shorter window). Away replies now go to **Ask every time** chats too (they're your own fixed text; Blocked chats still never get one). The limits are 20 replies in any hour and 30 in one window (it was 20 a night), never two to the same chat within 30 minutes, and a wait that ends late because the laptop slept sends nothing. An older `config.json` keeps its one window (18:00 to 09:00 unless it said otherwise), so upgrading never starts replying all day. `tgmcp away` lists every schedule; `tgmcp away <schedule> hours|message` changes one by name, and without a name, like the menu bar's Away page, the one replying now.
- **Tray: an Away tab built around schedules**: a top card with the master switch, what's replying now and until when, replies waiting out their wait, and today's timeline with a line at now. Each schedule is a card with its own switch; click it to edit the name, the hours (time pickers), the days (Every day / Weekdays / Weekends or any), the dates (a calendar picker), the wait, the repeat and the message with a preview, then **Save** or **Discard**. **Add** starts one blank or from a template (Lunch break, Weekend, Time off), and **Delete** asks once more.
- **Make the footer your own**: change the footer from the tray's new **Footer** tab (Ctrl+4) or with `tgmcp footer on|off|<text>`, as well as in `config.json`. Switching it off keeps its text for when you switch it back on. The tab has a live preview of a message with it, ready-made ideas, letter styles that look the same in every Telegram app (bold, italic, mono, small caps), the space above it, a counter and **Reset**. The daemon serves it on `POST /ui/footer` with the token; no MCP tool can change it. Control characters, text direction marks and invisible-only footers are refused. In markdown and html messages the footer is now escaped (it could turn bold or break the html before), a file caption the footer takes past 1024 characters is refused up front, and an edit after the footer changed gets the new footer instead of both.
- **Tray: both kinds of auto-reply on Home and Contacts**: Home's **Auto-reply** tile counts the chats with auto-reply on, and its chip says whether the away reply is off, on or replying now. Its section has the **away auto-reply switch** (on or off without opening the Away tab; **Hours and message** opens it) and **Auto-reply in chats**, every saved chat with its switch. On Contacts each row has an **Auto-reply** switch, a filter shows **All / Auto-reply on / Auto-reply off**, and an open contact's **Auto-reply On / Off** says what listening there comes to.
- **Tray: choose who Claude listens to, and one section at a time**: Home's tiles are now **Waiting / Listening / Sent / Auto-reply**, two by two, and the one you click is the section shown below them (a new draft switches back to Waiting). Under **Listening now** each watched chat has a switch to pause or resume it, and a session watching several saved chats has an **all** switch. A switch is the chat's auto-reply, so pausing stops its messages reaching every session at once while the watches stay.
- **Edits and deletes ask where sending does**: `telegram_edit_message` and `telegram_delete_message` no longer refuse in a chat where sending needs approval (Ask every time, a chat on no list, an auto-reply chat the session isn't watching, or a file in a watched chat). They wait for you like a send: the menu bar and tray show the chat, the message number and its text now, then the new text for an edit (**Deny** / **Edit**) or the message that would go for everyone for a delete (**Keep** / **Delete**), in the card and the notification. The Claude Code dialog asks the same, and without either the model gets an approval id for `telegram_confirm_send` / `telegram_cancel_send`. Deny, or no answer within 20 minutes, leaves the message as it was. Approving never puts a chat on a list, the rules are checked again when you approve (a chat blocked meanwhile is refused), and `daemon.log` records the ask and what came of it. Auto-send chats still change at once, blocked chats still refuse, and only a session's own recent messages can be changed, as before.

## 1.1.0

- **Who has read my message**: `telegram_message_readers` says whether one of your own sent messages has been read. In a private chat it says whether the other person has read it; in a group of up to 100 people it lists who read it and when, for about a week after it was sent. It only reports what Telegram tells your account, so people who hide their read receipts, bigger groups and channels aren't shown. It only reads: it sends, edits and deletes nothing.
- **Delete a sent message**: `telegram_delete_message` deletes, for everyone in the chat, a message the calling session sent recently (its sends, files and stickers, a `/reply` answer or an approved draft). It follows the edit rules: only that session's own messages (never the Page button's, the pager's, an away reply or another session's), and only where sending needs no approval (auto-send, a watched auto-reply chat or a `/reply` takeover). It can't be undone, so the tool says to use it only to take back something that shouldn't be there. The message leaves Recently sent and `daemon.log` records each delete.
- **Tray page alarm in the panel's look**: the Windows alarm window that rings for a page now matches the tray panel and the menu bar's alarm: borderless smoked glass (acrylic on Windows 11, the same tints opaque elsewhere) that you can drag anywhere, a bell in a red disc with "Someone needs you now", red-tinted cards with the avatar, "3m ago", the reason on a frosted inset, and **Ack** / **Snooze 15m** / **1h** with **Open** on the right. At most 3 cards show, then "+N more". Esc or the close button only hides it; it keeps ringing until acked or snoozed.
- **Sort and filter chats**: `telegram_list_chats` takes `type` (`all`, `private`, `groups`, `channels`, `bots`) to list one kind of chat, `sort` (`recent`, `unread`, `name`), and `categorize` to group the list under Private chats / Groups / Channels / Bots headings with counts and unread totals. Filtering looks further back through your dialogs so the limit still fills.
- **Profile photos on avatars**: the menu bar and tray apps show each chat's Telegram profile photo on Home's cards, in Contacts and on the page alarm, instead of only initials. The daemon fetches the small photo once, caches it in `avatars/` for a day and serves it on `GET /ui/avatars/:chatId` behind the same token as `/ui/state`, only for chats the apps show. The apps ask once per chat (again after 6 hours), and fall back to initials when there's no photo or it can't be loaded.
- **Tray initials skip the [nickname]**: "Leo Perera [leo]" shows "LP" in the tray too, not "LL".
- **Edit a sent message**: `telegram_edit_message` replaces the text of a message Claude sent earlier (by chat and message id), for a typo or an update. A session can only edit its own recent text messages (its sends, `/reply` answers and approved drafts, kept in `recent.json` across restarts), never another session's, yours (the Page button's included), the pager's or an away reply, and never a file or sticker. It only works where sending needs no approval right now (auto-send, a watched auto-reply chat, or a `/reply` takeover), because an edit changes what the chat already sees; blocked and ask-every-time chats are refused. The footer is added again, text starting with `/reply` is refused, and Telegram's 48-hour edit limit applies. Recently sent in the menu bar and tray marks an edited message, keeps the original text, and `daemon.log` has a line for each edit.
- **Menu bar panel redesign (#19)**: the macOS panel gets the tray's look (smoked glass, frosted cards, one orange accent, Lucide icons, the paper-plane logo) and a simpler flow. **Home / Contacts / Away** are tabs (⌘1–⌘3), so the header keeps only the status pill and **Rules** / **Log**, and a long "Connected as …" name is no longer cut off by the Away button. Home's **Waiting / Listening / Sent** tiles pick the one section shown below them. Drafts show "Asked by …" and the reason on separate lines, "just now" / "5m ago", and **Send**, or **Send, ask next time** / **Send, always allow** for a new chat (in notifications too). Failures flash red with a warning, text meets WCAG AA in both themes, and controls have VoiceOver names. The menu bar icon uses the tray's status colours: blue / yellow / red / grey plane, green listening dot, amber drafts badge. The page alarm is restyled and keeps its 3-card limit.
- **Choose who Claude listens to, from the panel**: in the menu bar app, every chat under **Listening now** has a switch to pause or resume it (and a session watching several chats has an **all** switch). **Chats Claude may listen to** lists every saved chat with its switch, and each Contacts row has a **Listen** switch and a **Listening / Not listening** filter. A switch is the chat's auto-reply, so pausing stops its messages reaching every session at once while the watches stay.
- **Away auto-reply**: off by default; when on, a friend who writes to you during your away hours (18:00 to 09:00 unless changed) gets one reply saying you're off for the night, with `{name}` as your first name and the footer added. It's for private chats only (never Blocked or Ask every time chats), once per chat per night and at most 20 a night, only while it's still away hours, quiet for 30 minutes after anything is sent from your account there (by you or by Claude, but not the Page button), skipped while Claude handles the chat or a page is open, and only for messages under 10 minutes old. Set it on the Away page of the menu bar app (macOS) or the Away tab of the tray app (Windows), with `tgmcp away [on|off|hours|message|quiet]`, or in `config.json`; no MCP tool can change it.
- **Tray panel redesign**: the Windows tray panel is now a frosted-glass flyout (Windows 11 acrylic, opaque on Windows 10 or with transparency off) with **Home** and **Contacts** tabs, a status pill, Waiting / Listening / Contacts tiles, cards with avatars, and empty states that say what each section is for. Drafts say plainly when it's the first message to a chat, and every contact setting has a one-line explanation. A new chat's choices say what they do (**Send, ask next time** / **Send, always allow**), failures flash red instead of with a check, text meets WCAG AA contrast in both themes, and every button can be reached with Tab and shows a focus ring. Keyboard: Esc, Ctrl+1 / Ctrl+2, Ctrl+F. The panel also opens at full width on a second monitor with other scaling (it used to be 2 px wide). Icons are [Lucide](https://lucide.dev) SVGs instead of font glyphs.
- **New icon and status colours**: the tray app uses a new paper-plane icon (`docs/icon.svg`) for the tray, the exe and notifications. The tray icon's colour shows the connection (blue online, yellow reconnecting, red logged out, grey offline), with a green dot while a session listens and an amber badge for drafts waiting. The panel uses the same colours.
- **Pages**: something that needs you now rings the menu bar app (macOS) or the tray app (Windows) with a looping alarm and a window above everything, until you Ack or Snooze it. Your own pager bot pushes it to your phone with the same buttons, and an Ack anywhere stops it everywhere. It's triggered by `/page <reason>` from people on `paging.pageFrom`, by keywords in `paging.vip` chats, or by Claude through `telegram_page`. The daemon decides what pages, the sender gets fixed replies ("Paging Leo now", "Leo saw it", "hasn't seen it yet, try calling"), and there's at most one page per sender every 10 minutes. `tgmcp page setup` connects the bot, and `tgmcp page test` rings a test page.
- **Paging from Contacts**: on the Contacts page of the menu bar and tray apps, **Can page me** puts a person on `paging.pageFrom` (or takes them off), and **Page** sends them `/page <reason>` from your account, so you can page someone in two clicks. Groups, bots, yourself and blocked chats are refused.
- **Tray panel stays above the taskbar**: on Windows the panel grew downward when its content did (opening Contacts, opening a contact, a long draft on hover), so it hung past the taskbar and off the screen, where the footer and the end of the list couldn't be reached. It's now anchored to the tray corner like the macOS panel: it grows upward, at most as tall as the screen, and the middle scrolls.
- **Tray panel scrolls with the wheel anywhere**: over a contact's nickname or page-reason box the wheel used to stop, because the text box took it. It now scrolls the panel wherever the pointer is.
- **Click to expand, never hover**: in the menu bar and tray apps, a long draft waiting for approval and a Recently sent chat used to pop open as the pointer passed over them. They now open on click (a long draft also gets **Show all**), and close on a second click.
- **Your own alarm sound**: `alarm.mp3` (or `.m4a`, `.wav`, `.aiff`, `.caf`) in the home folder replaces the built-in beep in the menu bar app, and `alarm.wav` does the same in the tray app.

## 1.0.0

The first release, with prebuilt binaries for macOS, Linux and Windows, the menu bar app for macOS and the tray app for Windows.

- **One always-on daemon** holds the only Telegram connection (your user account). Every Claude Code session talks to it through a thin MCP proxy, so sessions never fight over the login and never need `/mcp reconnect`. It reconnects by itself, catches up on missed messages, and `tgmcp install` keeps it running under launchd.
- **Rules file**: chats can be blocked, need approval, send without asking, or allow auto-replies, and nicknames make "send to leo" mean exactly one person. A first message to a new chat always asks, and your answer decides what happens next time.
- **Approvals** in the menu bar app (with notification actions), a Claude Code dialog, or in chat. Nothing goes out unless you accept.
- **Menu bar app**: approvals, which sessions are listening to which chats, the chats you last sent to, and a **Contacts** page to change each saved chat's nickname, sending rule and auto-reply without editing `config.json` (#3).
- **Windows tray app**, the menu bar app's counterpart, with the same panel, Contacts page and notifications (#4). The Windows release includes it as one self-contained exe next to `tgmcp.exe`, so it needs no .NET; `tgmcp menubar` starts it.
- **Watching**: `telegram_watch` pushes a chat's new messages into one or more sessions (Monitor, channel push or a queue) so they can reply while they listen.
- **Files**: `telegram_send_file` sends a local file under the same rules, and files to a chat a session is watching always ask (#1). `telegram_download_media` saves a message's attachment.
- **`/reply`**: type `/reply` in any chat from your own account, on your phone or desktop, to hand that chat to a Claude session for 2 hours. `/reply <what to say>` says what you want, and `/reply stop` takes it back. The session gets the chat's new messages and replies without approval. Blocked chats are never taken over, files still ask, and only a `/reply` you type counts, never one the daemon sent (#8).
- **Stickers**: `telegram_send_sticker` re-sends a sticker someone posted, by its Telegram file id, under the same rules as messages. Stickers can't carry a caption, so they go out without the footer (#6).
- **Secrets in the OS keystore**: the API token and api_hash live in the Keychain (Credential Manager, Secret Service), and the Telegram auth key in `session.db` is sealed with a key kept there.
- **Text keeps its leading spaces**, so indented messages arrive as written (#2).
- **`tgmcp version`**, and release builds that run without Bun or a checkout: `tgmcp install`, `tgmcp menubar` and `tgmcp mcp-add` use the installed binary and app.
